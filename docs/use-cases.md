# Use cases

Tandem is strongest when the value of an object comes from two people sharing authorship or stewardship over time.

These examples describe product possibilities. They are not claims that mainnet is active, that external content will remain available, or that Tandem guarantees legal ownership, identity, privacy, or financial value.

## A shared creative record

Two collaborators create one object for a body of work. Each major release adds a chapter. If responsibility changes, both authorize a new control pair. When the project ends, they close the object together.

Why Tandem fits: the history stays attached to one object and no participant can rewrite control alone.

## A long-running partnership archive

Two partners use chapters to commit milestone records over a long relationship. The content can remain encrypted while its exact bytes are authenticated by the commitment.

Why Tandem fits: the object begins with the pair and its sequence makes the shared history explicit.

## Rotating stewardship

Two stewards maintain an object for a team or collection. A planned transition rotates one or both keys with approval from the current pair.

Why Tandem fits: succession is an exact state transition, not an invisible account permission edit.

## A jointly closed chapter

Two participants create a finite object whose conclusion matters as much as its beginning. A cooperative close records the terminal state and pays the carrier value under exact rules.

Why Tandem fits: ending is part of the protocol, not an abandoned account.

## Poor fits

Tandem is not suitable when one participant must act unilaterally, when an administrator needs discretionary recovery, when the content must remain private without careful encryption and metadata planning, or when either participant cannot safely manage keys and recovery material.

It is also not a marketplace, token sale, yield product, ranking system, or human identity proof.
